   We will be hosting many different playing levels. Below you can find format-specific rules for each level.

Tier "C": This game will focus on having the least amount of rules to allow for more touches and rallies.


    Net Height:      224-228 cm
    Antennas:      No
    Ceiling obstructions:      Ok
    Hand touches of the net:      Ok
    Body touches of the net:      Ok
    Doubles/Lifts:      Ok
    Foot over centre line:      Ok
    Foot over service line:      Ok
    Throws:      Ok
    Serve re-try:      1 re-serve allowed
    Back row attacks:      No
    Setter dumps:      No
    Dedicated setter:      No, middle sets
    Dedicated middles:      No
    5-1 Rotation:      No
    Premade Teams:      No

Skill Guidelines:

Players who are new to the game should start volleyball as a "Beginner". Although there is no skill requirement for this game, it is expected that players have earned a basic understanding of the rules of Volleyball, how the game is played, how a point can be scored, how a match can be won, and some basic skills to promote rallies like bumping, setting and passing.


Tier "B": This game will require more precision in all areas of the game, still with a focus on improvement and rallies.


    Net Height:      235-238 cm
    Antennas:      Yes
    Ceiling obstructions:      Ok
    Hand touches of the net:      Only light touches
    Body touches of the net:      Only light touches
    Doubles/Lifts:      Ok, but obvious ones should be called
    Foot over centre line:      No
    Foot over service line:      No
    Throws:      No
    Serve re-try:      No
    Back row attacks:      Ok
    Setter dumps:      Ok, but only if ball is not settable
    Dedicated setter:      No, but front row setting is Ok
    Dedicated middles:      No
    5-1 Rotation:      No
    Premade Teams:      No

Skill Guidelines:

Players who are no longer considered new to the game should be labeled "Intermediate". They would have played some organized volleyball in elementary, highschool, or with friends. They know how to bump, set, and attack successfully. They know the rules, and are starting to learn advanced moves, and to pepper with a friend. They are starting to experiment with jump serves, spiking from back row, or becoming a full time positional player like setter or libero. The intermediate player knows how to execute skills, but not with consistency and accuracy. The player should be able to pass a "free ball" with ease when a "free ball" is struck over the net. Safety regarding spiking, blocking, footwork landing have between practised. "6 back" is not yet apparent to the Intermediate player, but is aware of positional requirements, where to stand, generally where to play on defense. Beginning to lose fear of the "fast spike", more willing to bump/dig hard balls sent at face level or near the torso. Does not double or lift the ball much while setting. Can serve the ball easily over the net and into player. at 235 cm, it should be easy for the Intermediate player to spike down into the other court. If this is not possible, the player is considered more a of a "Defensive" player, and should focus on these skills more than offensive skills. Wants to generally improve by playing 3-touch volleyball, does not willingly put the ball over on 1 or 2. Their serve is beginning to pick up in speed, and is not so easy to return everytime. Aware of the 3M line, has attempted to attack from back row and learn the positional timing. Starting to branch out into positions like "Middle", "Setter", "Opposite/Right Side", "Outside Hitter", "Libero". Hands are becoming strong and practising the "hand passing". Starting to communicate vocally on offense and defense.


Tier "A": This game will feature serious and competitive players. It is expected that all rules are followed, similar to a professional game.


    Net Height:      243 cm
    Antennas:      Yes
    Ceiling obstructions:      No
    Hand touches of the net:      No
    Body touches of the net:      No
    Doubles/Lifts:      No
    Foot over centre line:      No
    Foot over service line:      No
    Throws:      No
    Serve re-try:      No
    Back row attacks:      Ok
    Setter dumps:      Ok
    Dedicated setter:      Yes
    Dedicated middles:      Yes, only if 2 players agree, otherwise position is played based on rotational location on court
    5-1 Rotation:      Yes
    Premade Teams:      Ok

Skill Guidelines:

Players who have improved further beyond Intermediate, that played club up or through highschool, or played highschool, but did not play university volleyball are known as "Advanced". Players who have played University/College are considered "Varsity", and should be labeled as "Competitive". Competitive would be 1 level below Professional or Semi-Professional. Usually, these players do not play in public amateur leagues, so won't go over this skill level much, but they are considerably better than the "Advanced" player.

"Advanced" is where positional 5-1 games start to happen. Usually, a player has "decided" their position, and stick to this position while playing at this level.

The positions are: Setter, Middle, OH, OPP or RS, Libero. Setters have great hands, and usually take the 2nd ball to help "Set" for the final 3rd ball, the "Attack". The middle is usually tallest on the team, playing the middle position front row, helping the team with blocking the pins. The outside hitter (OH) is an offensive player that takes most of the serve receives, and attack from the pins and back row middle. The opposite or right side is usually the tall and star attacker, spiking from mainly the right side of the court. The libero is the defensive specialist, focused on serve receive, spike defense, and floor defense.

Many "Intermediate" players believe their skill is advanced, but usually it is not, and can ruin Advanced games. Therefore, at Airbase Volleyball, Advanced games will be invite-only in order to maintain the game integrity.

The advanced player (AP) can execute all skills an intemediate player can, but generally with more consistency, precison and power. The net height of 243 cm should not be a problem for the AP. The elbow joint should near the top of the net while blocking, and practise spikes should all land inside the court during hitting lines. An AP has developed a difficult-to-receive serve such as a jump top-spin or jump-float, and isn't afraid to hand pass hard-served balls. The AP knows about "6 back", because playing 6 up in a game of hitters means you will get hit in the face. The AP is always ready to play defense on a roll shot, lines up hitting shoulders vs. empty hitting lanes, and hugs the line when guarding line hits at position 1 or 5. An AP will never blame the setter for a bad set, because the AP has the footwork to adjust to a bad set, and attack. AP can pepper for 5 minutes straight without dropping the ball with an equal partner. In general, the AP is athletic, allowing them to block or hit at will, and stretch out for a pancake on the ground if needed. The AP doesn't get scared when a hard hit ball comes near their upper body, hard driven balls usually are defended well. The AP blocks with their hands pushed over the net, curling the hands in the direction of the court. The AP will almost 100% of the time pass a "dime" or 10 back to the setter when a free or soft ball is played on their side.

The AP is eager to play defense, because good defense means more rallies, and more rallies means more fun for everyone. The minimum requirements to play in the Advanced game at airbase volleyball is an outstanding ability to attack over the net, or an equal outstanding ability to play defense on the other end. We understand not everyone is going to have natural jumping ability, or the height to spike above 243 cm; the defensive abilities must make up for the offensive liabilities.


Tier "A/B": This game will feature a mix of men and women of ADVANCED skill, due to the high organization required for this format


General Rules


• The net height is always 224 cm
• The game will be played with 3 women and 3 men on each side
• Floor positions are always alternating between men and women, either MFMFMF or FMFMFM, 2 males or 2 females in the front row at one time
• Men are not allowed to jump serve, or have their feet in the air while the ball is being served
• If a team uses all 3 touches, 1 touch must be made from a female player
• Men may only attack from the back 3M line, and attacks cannot be dinks or soft arc touches, they must be forceful
• Only female players are allowed to block, men are not allowed to block or raise their hands higher than the height of the net
• If the rotation has only 1 female up front, 1 female from backrow may rotate up front; she can block, but she cannot attack unless its behind the 3M line
